# Methods, Books & Platforms

## What Piano Marvel actually got right

Worth naming precisely, because it tells us what to copy:

1. **Tiny steps with a lot of material at each one.** Hundreds of very short pieces per level, so you
   read *new* music every day instead of memorizing a few songs.
2. **A limited set of keys that grows slowly.** It starts with each hand in a fixed five-note position,
   the hands taking turns, then one hand holding notes while the other moves, then both moving.
3. **Immediate, objective feedback**: scoring of pitch and rhythm against a moving cursor.
4. **Sight-reading assessment (SASR)**: a level number that tells you how well you read, separate from
   how well you play pieces you've practiced.
5. **Technique as its own track**: scales, chords and arpeggios, levelled separately from pieces.

Items 3–4 are software, and you've already built a version. Items 1, 2 and 5 are **content and
sequencing**, which the rest of this file covers.

## The traditions in short

| Approach | Idea | Fit for you |
|---|---|---|
| **Middle C method** (Thompson, older Alfred) | Both thumbs on Middle C, learn notes by name, expand outwards | Easy start but tends to lock you into one position. Your books by Milnes and Ferrante are in this style |
| **Multi-key / intervallic** (Faber, Alfred Adult, Bastien) | Read by *shape*: steps, skips, intervals. Several five-finger positions early | ✅ Best for sight-reading; closest to Piano Marvel |
| **Graded exam syllabi** (ABRSM, RCM, Trinity) | Levelled lists of pieces, études, technique and sight-reading, from Prep to Grade 8/10 | ✅ The best yardstick from about year 1. Free syllabus PDFs; books are cheap |
| **Classical progressive études** (Beyer, Czerny, Bartók, Burgmüller) | 19th/20th-c. graded pieces written for teaching | ✅ Public domain or cheap, MusicXML available, perfect app content |
| **Chord/lead-sheet methods** (Pianote, Playground Sessions) | Chords and accompaniment patterns, reading comes second | ❌ Leans toward pop; skip as a main method |
| **Falling-notes apps** (Synthesia, Simply Piano, Flowkey visuals) | Follow lights or blocks | ❌ Doesn't teach reading. Fine as a toy, not as a method |

## Recommended spine

### Months 0–12: adult method + plenty of easy reading

- **Faber — *Adult Piano Adventures All-in-One Course, Book 1***, then **Book 2**.
  Adult pacing, intervallic reading, technique and theory included, and a lot of classical
  pieces simplified at the right level. Recommended over Alfred's *Adult All-in-One* because it introduces
  more positions and keys early. (Alfred's is a fine alternative if you find it cheaper.)
- **Bartók — *Mikrokosmos* Vol. 1 & 2** (153 pieces in total across 6 vols; Vol. 1–2 are beginner).
  Very short pieces that start with hands in unison, then contrary motion, then independent hands. This is
  the closest thing to Marvel's early levels in the classical repertoire, and it's real music, often
  modal, with a sound that suits your taste.
- **Beyer — *Elementary Method* Op. 101** (public domain). A 19th-c. step-by-step method with extremely
  limited notes at first. Good material for your app's early levels.
- **Paul Harris — *Improve Your Sight-reading!*** (Faber Music, Pre-Grade 1, then Grade 1…). Graded
  daily sight-reading drills.

### Month ~12 onwards: graded syllabus

Pick **one**:

- **RCM Celebration Series** (Preparatory A/B, Level 1–10, each with *Repertoire* and *Études* books, plus
  *Technical Requirements*). Very clear levels, and the syllabus is free from rcmusic.com.
- **ABRSM Piano Exam Pieces** (Initial, Grade 1–8, new books every two years) plus *Specimen Sight-Reading Tests*.

Use the level to judge any piece: if a neoclassical piece is rated "Grade 3", it belongs to the stage
where you're playing ABRSM Grade 3 / RCM Level 3 material.

### Your three books

| Book | Where it fits |
|---|---|
| Heather Milnes — *Learn to Play Piano* | Weeks 1–6. Middle C position, hands alternating, short tunes. A gentle first week or two before Faber, or for extra reading |
| Michael Williams — *Piano and Keyboard for Beginners* | A theory companion (keys, intervals, chords, scales, arpeggios, minor keys). Read a chapter when the syllabus reaches that topic. Not a playing method |
| Damon Ferrante — *Piano Book for Kids 5 and Up* | Very early extra reading only. You'll outgrow it in a few weeks |

| Book | Stage | Notes |

| *Discovering Music Theory* (ABRSM Grade 1–5 workbook) | 2–5 | ✅ Theory companion: do the chapter for your current grade alongside the pieces |
| ABRSM *Piano Exam Pieces 2025 & 2026*, Grade 1–8 | 3 → 5+ | ✅ The graded spine from Stage 3. Grade 1–2 → Stage 3 · Grade 3–4 → Stage 4 · Grade 5–6 → Stage 5 · Grade 7–8 → later. **Initial** grade (the step before Grade 1) is missing but optional |

**ABRSM pieces to skip under your no-pop rule** (all others in Grades 1–5 are original classical/educational works):
Grade 1 *Remember Me* (from *Coco*) · Grade 3 *Where is Love?* (*Oliver!*) and *The Muppet Show Theme* ·
Grade 4 *Wallace and Gromit Theme* · Grade 5 *Someone You Loved* (Capaldi, arr.) and *The Village in May* (Hisaishi, *Totoro*, arr.).
You only need one piece from each list (A/B/C), so skipping these costs nothing. I haven't checked Grades 6–8 yet.

Good picks in those books for your taste: Grade 2 Kabalevsky *A Little Song* · Grade 3 Bartók *Allegretto* (*For Children*), Bonis *Douce amie* ·
Grade 4 Grieg *Waltz* Op. 12/2, McDonagh *Cloudscapes* · Grade 5 Schubert *Waltz in B minor*, Prokofiev *La pluie et l'arc-en-ciel*.

The ABRSM 2025–2026 pieces can still be used for exams until the end of 2027. For practice they never expire.

> These PDFs came from z-library, so keep `literacy/` out of any public repo and never bundle them into the app.
> For the app, use public-domain sources (Beyer, IMSLP/OpenScore) or your own transcriptions.

## Without a teacher

The one thing an app can't catch is posture, wrist and hand-shape problems, which are hard to unlearn later.
The substitute: watch the posture videos in [08-videos.md](08-videos.md) in week 1, then **film yourself from the
side at the start of each stage** and compare against the checklist there.
