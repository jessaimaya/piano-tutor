# Syllabus

Six stages. Each stage moves forward on **five parallel tracks** at once, so no single skill
races ahead of the others (the main reason self-taught players stall).

| Track | What it trains |
|---|---|
| **R — Reading** | Note recognition on both staves, intervals, ledger lines, key signatures |
| **T — Time** | Pulse, note values, rests, meters, counting out loud, metronome |
| **C — Coordination** | How the two hands work together, from taking turns to fully independent |
| **K — Keyboard technique** | Hand shape, fingering, scales, chords, arpeggios, pedal, dynamics |
| **M — Music** | Real pieces, played musically, finished and kept up |

Durations assume 20–30 min/day. **Move on by exit criteria, not by the calendar.**
"Grade" refers to ABRSM/RCM levels, roughly.

---

## Stage 0 — Orientation (weeks 1–4)

*Book: Milnes p.1–20, then Faber Adult Book 1, Units 1–3. Videos: posture + hand shape ([08-videos.md](08-videos.md)).*

- **R** Key names and black-key groups · Middle C · treble C–G, bass C–F around Middle C · steps vs. skips
- **T** Quarter, half, dotted half, whole notes · 4/4 and 3/4 · count out loud · steady pulse at a slow tempo (♩=60)
- **C** **The hands take turns, never together**: the melody passes from LH to RH
- **K** Sitting height, curved fingers, finger numbers 1–5, playing from the arm rather than pressing with the finger
- **M** Short tunes from the method

**Exit:** Name any note in the Middle C range within 2 s · play a 16-bar hands-alternating piece without stopping, in time.

## Stage 1 — Five-finger positions (months 1–3)

*Faber Book 1 (rest of the first half). Mikrokosmos Vol. 1 nos. 1–20. Beyer Op. 101 opening exercises.*

- **R** Read by interval (2nd–5th) instead of naming every note · C, G and Middle-C positions · treble G–D above · bass F–C below
- **T** Eighth notes · quarter rests · ties · upbeats · metronome introduced
- **C** **First hands together**: (1) both hands in unison an octave apart → (2) one hand holds a long note while the other moves → (3) contrary motion
- **K** Five-finger patterns in C, G, F, D major · legato vs. staccato · *p* / *f*
- **M** First short "real" pieces (Mikrokosmos, easy Türk/Gurlitt in the method) · learn to *finish* a piece

**Exit:** Sight-read a Mikrokosmos Vol. 1 piece with ≥ 90 % notes and rhythm on the first try at a slow tempo · five-finger patterns HT in 4 keys.

## Stage 2 — Leaving the position (months 3–7)

*Faber Book 1 (second half). Mikrokosmos Vol. 1 end / Vol. 2 start. Paul Harris Sight-reading Pre-Grade 1.*

- **R** Ledger lines (to A above / E below) · sharps, flats, naturals · key signatures of C, G, F
- **T** Dotted quarter + eighth · 2/4 · simple syncopation · counting subdivisions ("1-&-2-&")
- **C** **Each hand doing its own job**: RH melody over held LH fifths or chords, then over a LH moving in quarter notes
- **K** Thumb-under and finger-over (crossing) · **one-octave scales HS**: C, G, F, A minor · triads I–IV–V in C/G · **sustain pedal intro** (pedal changes after each chord)
- **M** First pieces in your genre: **Pärt — *Für Alina*** (very few notes; trains pedal, timing and listening) · easy Bach Notebook pieces from the method

**Exit:** One-octave scales HT in C & G at ♩=60 in eighths · play a piece with pedal changes, keeping the sound clean · finish *Für Alina*.

## Stage 3 — Accompaniment patterns · ≈ Grade 1–2 (months 7–14)

*Faber Adult Book 2. Mikrokosmos Vol. 2. Beyer Op. 101 (later). From mid-stage: **ABRSM Grade 1, then Grade 2** books (in `literacy/`). Theory: Discovering Music Theory, Grade 1–2.*

This is the stage where neoclassical music opens up: almost all of it is **a melody over a broken-chord LH**.

- **R** Keys up to 2♯/2♭ · harmonic/natural minor · reading chord shapes at a glance · 6/8
- **T** Triplets · sixteenth notes in simple groups · steady pulse while the hands move independently
- **C** **Broken-chord LH patterns**: Alberti bass, 1-5-8 and 1-5-10 arpeggiated patterns, waltz bass; the RH keeps a melody going over them
- **K** Scales 2 octaves HT (C G D F + relative minors) · triads + inversions · **broken chords and short arpeggios** · **syncopated (legato) pedalling** · voicing: melody louder than accompaniment
- **M** First Einaudi originals: ***Dietro Casa***, ***Corale***, ***The Tower*** · Bach *Minuet in G* (it's in Williams ch. 20) · Kabalevsky Op. 39 selections

**Exit:** Paul Harris / app sight-reading test at Grade 1 sight-reading comfortably · LH arpeggio patterns at ♩=80 under a RH melody · 3 finished pieces kept up.

## Stage 4 — Real repertoire · ≈ Grade 3–4 (months 14–26)

***ABRSM Grade 3, then Grade 4** books + études (Burgmüller Op. 100, Czerny Op. 599 selections). Theory: Grade 3–4.*

- **R** All major/minor keys up to 4♯/4♭ · reading two voices in one hand
- **T** Rubato *with* a steady inner pulse · cross-rhythm basics (2 against 3) · long held notes
- **C** Larger LH leaps (bass note → chord) · melody and accompaniment *in the same hand* · hands crossing
- **K** Scales and arpeggios 2–4 octaves · dynamics as shaping (cresc./dim. over phrases) · half-pedal on digital pianos that support it · relaxation at speed
- **M** Satie ***Gymnopédie No. 1*** · Einaudi *The Easiest Original Pieces* (most of it) · easier pieces from the **Chad Lawson** and **Ólafur Arnalds — *some kind of peace*** songbooks · Burgmüller (*Arabesque*, *Candeur*) · Chopin *Prelude in E minor* Op. 28/4

**Exit:** Learn a new Grade 3 piece to performance level in ≤ 3 weeks · record 5 finished pieces.

## Stage 5 — Neoclassical core · ≈ Grade 5–6 (year 2–3+)

***ABRSM Grade 5–6** books. Bach Little Preludes. Glass/Pärt. Theory: Grade 5.*

- **R** All keys · reading quickly enough to learn pieces in larger chunks
- **T** Polyrhythm, rubato phrasing, playing along with recordings
- **C** Fully independent hands, fast LH figures (the *Comptine* / *Le Onde* type)
- **K** Speed with relaxation · octaves · pedal colour · tone control at *pp*
- **M** Einaudi ***Una Mattina***, ***Nuvole Bianche***, ***I Giorni*** · Tiersen ***Comptine d'un autre été***, ***La Valse d'Amélie*** · Arnalds ***Saman*** · Frahm *SHEETS Eins* (slower Wintermusik/Felt pieces) · Glass ***Opening*** · Chopin *Waltz in A minor* (B.150)

**Exit:** None. From here on it's choosing repertoire, using the level system to keep pieces within reach.

---

## Rules that apply to every stage

1. **Learn a piece hands separately only until each hand is secure, then go to HT slowly.** Don't polish one hand alone for weeks.
2. **Slow and correct beats fast and sloppy.** Use a metronome tempo where you make ≤ 1 mistake per pass.
3. **Count out loud** until you no longer need to.
4. **Sight-reading means: play it once, keep going, don't stop to fix mistakes.** Fixing is for practice pieces.
5. **Keep up 3–5 finished pieces.** Replay them weekly; they're what you actually *have* as a pianist.
6. **A neoclassical piece only counts as "within reach" if its grade is ≤ your current stage + 1.** Beyond that, it costs more motivation than it gives.
